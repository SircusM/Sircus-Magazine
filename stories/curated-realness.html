Authenticity is no longer discovered. It is designed.

An essay from Issue 16
October 2026

01 /

The Premise

Authenticity
Selection
Performance

A monument to manufactured honesty.

The poster towers above the cosmetics aisle, a monument to manufactured honesty. “This image has not been retouched,” it declares in clean sans-serif type, as if transparency were a brand promise rather than a moral imperative. Below the text, a model stares back with visible pores, a small scar threading through her eyebrow, stretch marks mapping the curve of her hip. She is stunning. Of course she is stunning.

This is the central contradiction of the authenticity economy: the belief that removing filters removes performance. That confession produces truth. That visibility guarantees honesty. It does not. The model in that campaign was not chosen despite her imperfections. She was chosen because of how precisely she carries them. Her scar reads as design. Her stretch marks resemble pattern. What appears unfiltered is, in fact, highly selected. The result is not reality, but a refined version of it.

Brands have learned that selling authenticity requires as much control as selling fantasy, often more. The visual language has shifted, but the intent has not. Where perfection once relied on polish, it now relies on restraint. The influencer posting a no-makeup selfie understands lighting, angle, and composition as well as any studio photographer. The image may reject overt artifice, but it is still constructed. It must be. Content that circulates widely is never accidental.

“What is shown as truth is still a constructed decision.”
