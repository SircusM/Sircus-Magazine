Crosses
& Cleavage.

On the cross as a fashion accessory, and what happens when conviction becomes optics.

An essay from Issue 13
November 2025

01 /

The Premise

Conviction
Display
Optics

Worn to be read.
Chosen to be seen.

For most of its history, the cross was worn to be read. It hung at the throat as a profession of faith, modest in scale and plain in meaning. It now arrives in sterling and pavé, sized for the camera and set against bare skin, and it is read very differently.

Crosses & Cleavage follows the symbol from the sanctuary to the spotlight. The question at its center is not whether the cross belongs in fashion. It is a harder one: once a sacred sign is chosen for the way it frames a neckline, whom is it addressing, and what does the wearer surrender in the exchange?

“It is about optics.”
